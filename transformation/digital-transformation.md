# Leading a Multi-Year Digital Transformation

## Context

I joined an international manufacturing and distribution organisation with operations across Australia, New Zealand, the UK and the US.

The business had grown around a relatively fragmented technology environment. Legacy systems, infrastructure and processes had accumulated over time, while the organisation's growth ambitions were creating new demands on technology.

I was brought in to modernise the environment and build the technology capability required to support that growth.

Over time, I grew the technology team from 2 to approximately 10 people supporting around 200 staff internationally.

---

## The Challenge

This wasn't a single technology project.

The environment included:

- Legacy infrastructure
- ERP and warehouse systems
- CRM
- Marketing platforms
- Data integration
- Websites and eCommerce
- Telecommunications
- Cybersecurity
- Business continuity
- Reporting and analytics

Improving one area without considering the others would simply move the problem somewhere else.

The challenge was creating a practical sequence for modernisation while the business continued to operate.

---

## Creating the Roadmap

I developed a five-year digital transformation roadmap that connected technology investment to the organisation's broader growth strategy.

Rather than treating every system as an isolated project, the roadmap considered the dependencies between:

**Infrastructure → Cloud → Business Systems → Data → Integration → Customer Experience → Security**

This also created a clearer basis for prioritising investment and explaining technology decisions to the business.

---

## Building the Technology Function

Transformation required capability as well as technology.

The internal technology team grew from approximately 2 to 10 people, supported by local, offshore and specialist partners.

This allowed the operating model to evolve alongside the technology environment.

The objective wasn't necessarily to bring everything in-house.

It was to understand which capabilities needed to exist internally and where external partners could provide better scale or specialist expertise.

---

## Cloud & Infrastructure

A major part of the program was moving the organisation toward a cloud-first approach.

This included adoption and expansion of:

- Microsoft Azure
- Microsoft 365
- Teams
- Intune
- Cloud security capabilities
- Cloud backup and recovery
- Modern networking
- Remote access
- Telephony and video conferencing

More than 30 systems were ultimately retired or consolidated as the environment was simplified.

This contributed approximately $150K in savings while also reducing technology complexity.

---

## Business Systems

Technology modernisation also required significant change across core business applications.

The transformation included work across:

### ERP & Supply Chain

Management and integration of ERP, warehouse management, logistics, 3PL and EDI environments.

### CRM

Reimplementation of Microsoft Dynamics CRM supporting sales, customer service and broader customer engagement.

### Marketing Technology

Integration of CRM with marketing automation, websites, eCommerce and digital channels.

### Data

Improving master data, consolidating disparate datasets and building integrations between ERP, CRM, Microsoft 365, Azure and SQL environments.

---

## Customer Experience

One of the objectives was to improve the way technology supported the customer lifecycle.

CRM, marketing automation, customer service, knowledge management, telephony and reporting were increasingly connected to provide better visibility of customer interactions.

This helped move technology beyond traditional back-office infrastructure and closer to revenue-generating business processes.

---

## Delivery

A transformation program of this size required more than selecting technology.

It involved:

- Business cases
- Investment prioritisation
- Project governance
- Vendor selection
- Risk management
- UAT and OAT
- Change and release management
- Data migration
- Go-live planning
- Hypercare
- Transition to BAU

I also introduced greater use of Agile and Minimum Viable Product thinking where appropriate.

This wasn't about following a methodology for its own sake.

It was about reducing the time between identifying a business problem and delivering something useful.

---

## Security & Resilience

Security and resilience were incorporated into the transformation rather than treated as separate projects.

This included:

- Essential Eight alignment
- ISO 27001 practices
- Penetration testing
- Security awareness
- Business Continuity
- Disaster Recovery
- Infrastructure resilience
- Zero Trust principles

Modernisation needed to make the environment both easier to operate and more resilient.

---

## Working With the Business

Some of the most difficult parts of transformation were not technical.

Legacy technology often exists because it supports an established business process, and changing the system usually means changing the way people work.

A significant part of my role therefore involved building support for changes that had previously been difficult or contentious.

That required explaining:

- Why change was necessary
- What business problem we were solving
- What the investment would deliver
- What risks were involved
- What would change for employees

Technology transformation ultimately depends on people accepting and adopting the change.

---

## Outcome

The program moved the organisation toward a substantially more modern, integrated and cloud-oriented technology environment.

It included:

- A five-year technology roadmap
- Growth of the technology function
- Cloud-first adoption
- Retirement or consolidation of 30+ systems
- Approximately $150K in savings
- ERP and supply-chain modernisation
- CRM transformation
- Marketing technology integration
- Modern collaboration and communications
- Improved data integration
- Stronger cybersecurity and resilience practices

---

## What I Took From It

Large transformation programs can easily become collections of technology projects.

The role of technology leadership is to keep bringing those projects back to the reason they exist.

For me, the most useful questions remained:

**What business capability are we improving?**

**What complexity can we remove?**

**What risk are we reducing?**

**And what will be measurably better when we're finished?**

A technology roadmap becomes valuable when the business can see itself in it.
