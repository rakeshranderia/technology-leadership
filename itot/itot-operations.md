# IT/OT: Managing Technology Where Downtime Has Real-World Consequences

## Context

Across several industrial and manufacturing environments, my technology responsibilities have extended beyond traditional corporate IT into systems supporting operational technology.

This included technology associated with SCADA and PLC environments, with my responsibilities focused on areas such as:

- Availability
- Networking
- Network segmentation
- Cybersecurity
- Patching
- Infrastructure
- Vendor management

I was not the controls engineer responsible for programming PLCs or designing production processes.

My role was ensuring that the technology surrounding and supporting these environments was reliable, secure and appropriately managed.

---

## Why OT Is Different

Many of the principles used in corporate IT still apply to operational technology.

Systems need to be:

- Available
- Secure
- Supported
- Monitored
- Recoverable
- Appropriately maintained

What changes is the context.

A failed corporate application may prevent someone from completing an administrative task.

A technology failure in a production environment can stop manufacturing, disrupt supply chains, damage equipment or potentially contribute to a safety issue.

That changes how technology risk needs to be considered.

---

## Availability Comes First

In a production environment, availability can be one of the most important technology requirements.

Changes that would be relatively straightforward in a corporate environment may require considerably more planning when they affect production systems.

This means understanding:

- Production schedules
- Maintenance windows
- Operational dependencies
- Recovery requirements
- Vendor availability
- Business continuity
- Safety implications

The technically ideal time to make a change is not always the operationally acceptable time.

---

## Cybersecurity Without Losing Sight of Operations

Operational environments also create a different cybersecurity challenge.

Improving security cannot be approached without understanding the potential operational consequence of the control.

Activities such as:

- Patching
- Network changes
- Access restrictions
- Endpoint controls
- Segmentation
- Remote access changes

may all be appropriate from a security perspective.

But they need to be implemented with an understanding of the production environment they support.

The objective is not security at any cost.

It is reducing cyber risk while maintaining safe and reliable operations.

---

## Network Segmentation

Networking is an important part of separating corporate and operational environments.

Segmentation can help reduce unnecessary connectivity and limit the potential impact of a security event.

However, effective segmentation also requires understanding legitimate operational dependencies.

Production environments may rely on communication between:

- SCADA systems
- PLCs
- Engineering workstations
- Servers
- Vendor systems
- Business applications
- Reporting platforms

The challenge is therefore not simply blocking connectivity.

It is understanding what connectivity is genuinely required and controlling it appropriately.

---

## Patching & Lifecycle Management

Patching is another area where corporate IT practices cannot always be applied unchanged.

Production technology may have:

- Restricted maintenance windows
- Vendor certification requirements
- Legacy operating systems
- Specialist applications
- Hardware dependencies
- Limited opportunities for downtime

This requires a risk-based approach.

Where immediate remediation is not practical, the discussion needs to consider compensating controls, segmentation, monitoring and an appropriate remediation plan.

---

## Vendor Management

Specialist vendors are often an important part of operational technology environments.

Technology leadership therefore needs visibility of:

- Vendor access
- Support arrangements
- Remote connectivity
- Maintenance responsibilities
- Escalation paths
- System dependencies

The existence of a specialist vendor does not remove the organisation's responsibility for understanding and managing the technology risk.

---

## Working Across IT and Operations

One of the most important aspects of IT/OT management is recognising that neither technology nor operations can manage the environment effectively in isolation.

Technology teams understand areas such as:

**Networks + Security + Infrastructure + Identity + Risk**

Operational teams understand:

**Production + Equipment + Process + Safety + Operational Impact**

Specialist vendors bring another layer of expertise.

Effective management requires those groups to work together.

---

## Human Safety

Operational technology also introduces something that can be less visible in traditional corporate IT:

**human safety.**

Where technology supports machinery or production processes, technology decisions need to be cognizant of their potential impact beyond data and system availability.

This does not make the IT function responsible for engineering safety.

It does mean technology leaders need to understand when a technology change has operational or safety implications and ensure the appropriate operational and engineering stakeholders are involved.

---

## My Approach

My approach to IT/OT has therefore been based on a relatively simple principle:

**Understand the production consequence before making the technology decision.**

That means considering:

**Availability + Security + Safety + Operational Impact + Recoverability**

rather than assessing a technology change purely through the lens of corporate IT.

---

## What I Took From It

IT and OT are becoming increasingly connected, which makes traditional organisational boundaries less useful.

Technology teams cannot ignore OT because it is "owned by engineering".

Equally, corporate IT practices cannot simply be imposed on production environments without understanding their operational consequences.

The technology leader's role is to help bridge that gap.

The goal is a production environment that is:

**Available enough to operate, secure enough to protect, and managed with an understanding of the people and processes that depend on it.**
